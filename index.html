Your Complete Travel Guide

Welcome to Zhangjiajie

Zhangjiajie is a city in northwestern Hunan Province, Central China — about 1,000 km (620 miles) from Shanghai and 1,500 km (930 miles) from Beijing. It is home to the Wulingyuan Scenic Area, a UNESCO World Heritage Site famous for more than 3,000 quartz-sandstone pillars rising sharply from misty valleys. These otherworldly peaks are what inspired the floating "Hallelujah Mountains" in the film Avatar.

Most international travelers spend 3–4 days to see the highlights. But with so many scattered viewpoints, cable cars, and shuttle routes, choosing the right attractions is harder than it looks. This guide compares every major spot, shows how much time you really need, and helps you build an itinerary that fits your travel style.

#1 Most Popular

Yuanjiajie

Home of the Avatar Hallelujah Mountain — a 1,080 m quartz pillar that looks exactly like the film's floating peaks. Ride the record-breaking Bailong Elevator (326 m) up the cliff face.

3–4 hrs • Don't miss the Misty Pavilion viewpoint

Sea of Clouds

Tianzi Mountain

At 1,262 m, this is the best place to witness an ocean of clouds rolling between the peaks. The Imperial Brush Peak and the sunrise viewpoint are unforgettable.

3–4 hrs • Arrive before dawn for sunrise

Easy Hike

Golden Whip Stream

A gentle 7.5 km walk along a crystal-clear stream, flanked by 350 m cliffs and ancient forest. Meet wild macaques along the way — the most relaxing walk in the park.

2–3 hrs • Flat, family-friendly trail

Panorama

Huangshizhai

Locals say "No visit to Zhangjiajie is complete without Huangshizhai." A loop trail (or cable car) delivers 360° panoramas of the densest cluster of pillars.

2–3 hrs • Best by cable car up, walk down

Off the Beaten Path

Yangjiajie

Famous for the Natural Great Wall — a ridge of upright stone columns that looks man-made. Far fewer crowds, perfect for hikers who want the park to themselves.

2–3 hrs • Quietest spot in Wulingyuan

Separate Day Trip

Tianmen Mountain

Outside the core park: ride the world's longest cable car, walk the 999-step Heavenly Stairway, and test your nerves on the glass skywalk.

Half–full day • Plan as a separate visit

When to Go

Best Time to Visit Zhangjiajie

Zhangjiajie is beautiful year-round — each season paints the peaks a different way.

Spring

Mar – May

New green growth, misty valleys, fewer crowds. Comfortable 15–25°C.

Summer

Jun – Aug

Lush and vibrant, best cloud seas after rain. Warm 25–35°C, peak season.

Autumn

Sep – Nov

Crisp air, golden foliage, clearest views. The most recommended season.

Winter

Dec – Feb

Snow-capped pillars, magical and quiet. Cold 0–10°C, lowest prices.

Our tip: April–May and September–October offer the best balance of weather and views. July–August is lively but humid and busy; winter is stunning after snow and the cheapest time to visit. Whenever you come, we'll tailor the route to the conditions.

Transportation

How to Get to Zhangjiajie

Easy to reach by air or high-speed rail, then simple shuttles inside the park.

By Air

Zhangjiajie Hehua Airport (DYG) has direct flights from major Chinese hubs — Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Xi'an, and Chongqing — plus seasonal international charters. The airport is ~40 min from the park entrance.

By Train

Zhangjiajie West Railway Station connects via high-speed rail from Changsha (≈2.5 hrs), Wuhan, and Guangzhou. A scenic and comfortable option — and the station is a short transfer to the Wulingyuan area.

Getting Around the Park

Inside Wulingyuan, a free shuttle-bus network links the main areas (Yuanjiajie, Tianzi, Golden Whip). Cable cars, the Bailong Elevator, and the small train at Ten-Mile Gallery cover the rest. With a private guide and driver, you skip the queues and see more in less time.

Good to Know

Travel Tips & Essentials

A few things that make your Zhangjiajie trip smoother.

Visa & Entry

Many nationalities qualify for China's 144-hour visa-free transit or a tourist (L) visa. We can advise based on your route — just ask on WhatsApp.

Money

Bring some cash (RMB) for small vendors and temple donations; cards and mobile pay work in hotels and big shops. We'll help with currency tips on arrival.

What to Pack

Comfortable walking shoes are essential. Layers for changing mountain weather, a light rain jacket, and a power bank for all the photos you'll take.

Best Photo Spots

Sunrise at Tianzi Mountain, the misty Avatar Platform in Yuanjiajie, and the reflections along Golden Whip Stream. Your guide knows the timing.

Language

English is limited outside top hotels. That's exactly why a private English-speaking guide makes all the difference — from ordering food to reading signs.
